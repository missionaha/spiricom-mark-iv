What this recreates

The audio generator and recording stage of Mark IV. The original radio path requires external hardware.

Original signal path

13-tone generator → AM RF signal generator → transmitting antenna → receiving antenna → AM receiver → speaker → room → microphone → recorder.

The manual describes a 29–31 MHz carrier and two physical gaps: antenna to antenna, and speaker to microphone. This app neither transmits nor receives that radio carrier.

The tones here are sine waves at 131, 141, 151, 241, 272, 282, 292, 302, 415, 443, 515, 653, and 701 Hz. Equal amplitudes and zero starting phase are reconstruction choices, not recovered historical settings.

Recording & privacy

Microphone access starts only after you press Record. We request that echo cancellation, noise suppression, and automatic gain control be disabled; your device may still apply processing. Actual browser-reported settings are included in the session log.

No account, upload, voice generation, speech recognition, or word bank. Audio is processed on this device. A recording does not by itself identify the source of a sound.

Connecting external equipment

Use a suitable audio output/interface to feed an external instrument's audio modulation input. Match input levels to that instrument's specifications. The browser supplies audio only; radio hardware and its operating requirements are separate.

This is an experimental reconstruction, not a verified method of communicating with the deceased.

Source: Metascience Foundation, Spiricom Technical Manual (1982), Mark IV description and Fig. 11, PDF pages 32–34. No dependencies or remote audio services.
